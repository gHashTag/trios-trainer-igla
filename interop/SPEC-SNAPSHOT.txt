//! Checkpoint save/load. Stored under `${TRIOS_CHECKPOINT_DIR}/{run-name}/{step}.bin`.
//! Resume sets `step_done` and re-attaches optimizer state.
//!
//! P4 addition: `ema_average` for post-hoc EMA over last N checkpoints.
//! Reference: Sanyal et al. 2024 — free generalization gain at zero training cost.
//!
//! # EPIC-446 — the on-disk artifact format (version 1)
//!
//! A checkpoint file is `${TRIOS_CHECKPOINT_DIR:-checkpoints}/{run}/{step}.bin`.
//! Every multi-byte integer and float is LITTLE-ENDIAN; floats are stored as
//! their IEEE-754 bit patterns via `to_le_bytes`, so NaN payloads and signed
//! zeros round-trip exactly. No padding, no alignment, no compression, no
//! trailer. Three sections:
//!
//! ```text
//!   header (152 bytes) | tensor directory (152 bytes) | payload
//! ```
//!
//! Header, at absolute byte offsets:
//!
//! ```text
//!    0   8  magic = b"TRIOSCKP"
//!    8   4  format_version: u32 = 1
//!   12   4  header_len: u32 = 152 (absolute offset of the tensor directory)
//!   16   4  vocab      20   4  dim         24   4  num_ctx
//!   28   4  hidden     32   4  d_model     36   4  num_heads
//!   40   4  attn_cfg_seq_len                44   4  num_attn_layers
//!   48   4  ngram      52   4  tensor_count = 19
//!   56   8  qk_gain: f64 bits
//!   64   8  attn_cfg_lr: f64 bits (HybridAttnConfig::lr, NOT the training lr)
//!   72   4  train_lr: f32 bits    76   4  attn_scale: f32 bits
//!   80   4  attn_seq: u32
//!   84  24  ctx_weights: [f32; 6] bits, index order 0..5
//!  108   8  seed: u64            116   8  step: u64
//!  124   1  gf16_enabled: u8     125   1  data_synthetic: u8
//!  126   2  reserved (must be zero, rejected if nonzero)
//!  128   8  optimizer: ASCII, NUL-padded
//!  136  16  fake_quant_format: ASCII, NUL-padded
//! ```
//!
//! The tensor directory is 19 consecutive `u64` element counts (f32 counts,
//! not byte counts) in the canonical order below. It is purely redundant with
//! the header scalars; it exists so that a load which would produce a
//! differently shaped model fails loudly instead of reshaping silently.
//!
//! Canonical tensor order (MUST NOT CHANGE within version 1):
//!
//! ```text
//!   0 embed  1..6 ctx[0..5]  7 proj  8 attn_down  9 attn_up  10 lm_head
//!  11 wq  12 wk  13 wv  14 wo  15 wq2  16 wk2  17 wv2  18 wo2
//! ```
//!
//! Note that `attn_down` (8) and `attn_up` (9) have identical element counts,
//! so the directory cannot detect a swap between them: canonical-order
//! discipline is the only protection there.
//!
//! `file_len = 304 + 4 * sum(directory counts)`.
//!
//! # Why a hand-rolled binary format
//!

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The sidecar record: `<step>.json` beside `<step>.bin`
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One JSON object per checkpoint, written whether or not a ledger database is
reachable. Its `schema` field carries a tag of the form
`trios-checkpoint-record/N`. The versions were intended to be PURELY ADDITIVE
from /2 onward, so that a newer reader loads an older record and an older reader
loads a newer one. THEY ARE NOT, and the exceptions are marked "retired" below:
/6 stopped writing best_val_bpb and /7 stopped writing run_id. A reader that
believes the additive claim will misreport those two names.

  schema  added
  ------  -------------------------------------------------------------------
   /1     canon_name, seed, step, path, sha256, bytes, format_version, hidden,
          d_model, num_attn_layers, optimizer, fake_quant_format,
          data_synthetic, bpb, ema_bpb, git_sha, git_dirty, corpus, run_id,
          ledger, ts
   /2     steps_total, gf16_floor_every, eval_every, git_provenance;
          renamed bpb -> final_val_bpb and added best_val_bpb;
          widened git_dirty to nullable
   /3     lr, attn_scale, attn_seq, platform, source_sha256
   /4     trainer (path, sha256, provenance), vocab
   /5     gf16_enabled
   /6     eval_chunks, eval_tokens, eval_seq (the grid the metric was read on),
          val_bpb_stderr, optimizer_params (beta1, beta2, eps, weight_decay,
          source);
          RETIRED best_val_bpb, replaced by min_observed_val_bpb
   /7     inside platform: rustflags_sha256, rustflags_source,
          source_digest_scope, remap_applied;
          RETIRED run_id

The /6 and /7 rows were derived by diffing the records under checkpoints/
against each other on 2026-08-03, not from the encoder and not from any earlier
prose. Every name in them is present in EVERY record on disk carrying that tag
or a higher one; a name held by only some records of a version is not listed as
that version's addition, because version inference must not depend on which run
happened to write it.

DRIFT OBSERVED, recorded because a reader has to handle it: platform's
source_digest_scope is listed at /7 because every /7 record carries it, but
three records on disk carry it under a /6 TAG - checkpoints/trios-train-rng47/
2000.json and both of checkpoints/loc-v3/ - written 2026-08-03 08:24-08:33 UTC,
about an hour before the first /7 tag at 09:25 UTC. The field shipped before its
version did. A conforming reader reports a later-schema field found under an
earlier tag rather than ignoring it or failing on it: the record is not lying
about anything, the tag is simply younger than the field.

Fields with a counterpart in the 152-byte header, and therefore cross-checkable
against the container by a reader holding both halves:

  sidecar field    header offset  header type
  --------------   -------------  --------------------------------------------
  sha256           (whole file)   SHA-256 of every byte of the .bin
  bytes            (whole file)   file length
  format_version    8   4         u32
  vocab            16   4         u32                        (schema 4)
  hidden           28   4         u32
  d_model          32   4         u32
  num_attn_layers  44   4         u32
  lr               72   4         f32 bits, train_lr         (schema 3)
  attn_scale       76   4         f32 bits                   (schema 3)
  attn_seq         80   4         u32                        (schema 3)
  seed            108   8         u64
  step            116   8         u64
  gf16_enabled    124   1         u8                         (schema 5)
  data_synthetic  125   1         u8
  optimizer       128   8         ASCII, NUL-padded
  fake_quant_format 136 16        ASCII, NUL-padded

Fields with NO counterpart in the container. A reader holding the bytes can
report their presence and their value but cannot confirm or refute either:
`platform` and `source_sha256` (schema 3), `trainer` (schema 4), the eval grid
and optimizer fields of schema 6 (`eval_chunks`, `eval_tokens`, `eval_seq`,
`val_bpb_stderr`, `optimizer_params`, `min_observed_val_bpb`), the whole of
schema 7 (`platform.rustflags_sha256`, `platform.rustflags_source`,
`platform.source_digest_scope`, `platform.remap_applied`), and every
schema /1 and /2 field not listed in the table above - notably `final_val_bpb`,
`best_val_bpb` and `ema_bpb`, which are measurements the container does not
carry, and `corpus`, `git_sha`, `git_dirty`, `git_provenance`, `steps_total`,
`gf16_floor_every`, `eval_every`, `run_id`, `ledger`, `ts`, `canon_name`,
`path`.

The count matters and is not flattering. A /1 record has 22 top-level keys and
11 of them are checkable against the container. A /7 record has 39 and the
checkable set is still 16: /6 added five fields and /7 four, and not one of the
nine has a counterpart in the bytes. Every schema bump since /5 has widened the
part of the record that must be taken on trust. `source_sha256` in particular
states a digest and a scope name (`platform.source_digest_scope`) but not the
file list, the ordering or the hashing rule, so no second implementation can
recompute it - it is an assertion, not a check.

A conforming reader must therefore state the scope of its own verdict: which
fields it compared against the container, which it merely echoed, and which it
had no meaning for at all. A bare "agrees on N fields" is a claim with an
unstated denominator.

Note that `vocab` (offset 16) and `gf16_enabled` (byte 124) were ALREADY in the
hashed header before schemas 4 and 5 existed. Those versions did not change a
single artifact byte; they surfaced in the evidence record two quantities that
had already decided the artifact's identity. That is why the container's
`format_version` is deliberately not bumped in step with the record schema:
bumping it would invalidate every archived artifact hash for a JSON field.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reading rule for the schema tag
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A tag is a CLAIM about a record; the fields are the record. A conforming reader
therefore dispatches on FIELD PRESENCE and treats the tag as one more assertion
to check, with three outcomes:

  1. Tag <= the highest version the reader knows, and every field of every
     version up to the tag is present: read and cross-check normally.
  2. Tag names a version whose fields are missing (whatever the tag's number):
     FAIL. The record over-promises about its own content. A defaulted value is
     not a measurement, and an absent field is not a zero.
  3. Tag ABOVE the highest version the reader knows, with every field of that
     highest known version present: PASS, with a note naming both versions. The
     record carries fields whose meaning the reader does not have; that is a
     difference in instrument age, not a defect in the artifact, and failing it
     would make every future record unreadable by every deployed reader.

Case 3 is the difference between a format that can be audited for a decade and
one that breaks at the next additive change.

Case 3 has a failure mode of its own, and it is quieter than case 2. A reader
whose table is merely STALE takes branch 3 on every newer record and passes it,
reporting agreement over only the fields it happens to know. That is how
`interop/triosckp_reader.py` behaved against /6 and /7 records while its table
stopped at /5: PASS, with a note about instrument age, and not one word about
`eval_chunks` or `rustflags_sha256`. The rule is right; a stale table makes it
report a shrinking fraction of the record as if it were the whole. Hence a
fourth requirement, orthogonal to the three cases above:

  4. Any key the reader has no meaning for - top level or nested - is reported
     BY NAME as present and not interpreted. Never a silent pass. A field the
     instrument cannot see is a field it cannot agree or disagree about, and an
     instrument that passes everything it cannot see is not a second opinion.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Exit codes of `ckpt_replay`, the reference grader
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`src/bin/ckpt_replay.rs` is this repository's own falsifier: it re-derives an
artifact from the sidecar that describes it and grades the result. Its exit code
IS the verdict, and a third party wiring it into their own audit needs the whole
table, not the two codes they happen to hit. `ckpt_replay --help` prints the same
list; if the two ever disagree, the binary is right and this file is stale.

  exit 0  VERIFIED. The bytes re-derived from the record's own parameters, and
          the metric was confirmed too when the record states one. Scoped to ONE
          platform triple, which is printed with the verdict.
  exit 1  MISMATCH, ARTIFACT ALTERED, ARTIFACT MISSING, CORPUS MISMATCH,
          TRAINER MISMATCH. The evidence and the re-derivation disagree; for the
          last three, nothing was executed at all.
  exit 2  INCOMPARABLE. The record cannot state its own recipe, or it
          contradicts the header inside the artifact it describes. This is a
          verdict, not a failure: the run may have been perfectly reproducible
          and its record simply does not say so.
  exit 3  REFUSED. The replay would cost more than `--max-steps` authorised.
          Nothing was executed.
  exit 4  ERROR. The check itself could not be run, so there is no verdict to
          record. Never a statement about the artifact.
  exit 5  BPB MISMATCH, BPB NOT CONFIRMED, GRID MISMATCH. The weights
          re-derived exactly and the NUMBER the record certifies did not, or was
          not gradeable against the replay.
  exit 6  L2 PASS / L3 FAIL. The bytes did NOT re-derive and the metric did,
          within a tolerance the binary prints (`--bpb-tolerance`, default
          0.005 bpb, derived from the paired cross-laboratory deltas published
          in docs/REPRODUCIBILITY-GRADING.md). This is the shape an honest
          second laboratory takes on another platform, and it is deliberately
          NOT exit 1: an instrument whose only outcomes are "bit-identical" and
          "indistinguishable from fraud" has no false-positive control.

A BPB verdict is scoped to the EVAL GRID the record declares. `final_val_bpb` is
a reading taken over `eval_chunks` windows of `eval_seq` tokens (schema 6, and
movable at training time through `TRIOS_EVAL_CHUNKS`), so a replay on a different
grid measures a different quantity: `ckpt_replay` drives the child to the
recorded grid, reads back the grid the replay actually used from the replay's own
sidecar, and reports any difference as GRID MISMATCH rather than as a claim that
the record misstated its number. A record from a schema older than /6 declares no
grid; those runs predate the knob and are replayed at the default, which is
stated in the verdict rather than assumed away.
